# COUNTRY GM — Bolt Food Malta

### I applied to help operate Bolt Food Malta. So I tried operating it first.

**Live product:** https://country-gm-bolt.vercel.app/

---

## The premise

I could send another application explaining that I can think strategically, work through ambiguity, use data, build systems and execute.

Instead, I wanted to show how I would approach the market.

So I built **COUNTRY GM**.

An experimental AI-native operating layer for Bolt Food Malta that asks a simple question:

> **If I were responsible for growing Malta profitably, what would I want to know on Monday morning — and what would I actually do about it?**

This is not a recreation of Bolt's internal systems.

I have no access to Bolt's internal data.

The product uses **public information, public signals, explicit inference and modeled assumptions** to demonstrate an operating approach.

---

# Monday, 08:03

COUNTRY GM starts by refusing to show everything.

It investigates the market and asks:

**What actually deserves management attention this week?**

The current public-data investigation produces three starting hypotheses:

### 01 — Own the First Night

Can Bolt turn a visitor's arrival in Malta into their first food order?

Malta receives millions of visitors annually.

Bolt already participates in multiple local mobility and delivery moments.

The hypothesis:

**Arrival → accommodation → hunger is an observable customer journey worth investigating.**

---

### 02 — Win Saturday 19:30

Bolt has publicly reported Saturday as Malta's strongest food-delivery day, with ordering peaking around 19:30.

That creates a marketplace question:

> What happens when maximum demand collides with merchant preparation, courier liquidity and customer expectations?

Instead of immediately recommending blanket courier incentives, COUNTRY GM forces the Growth, Courier, Experience and Economics perspectives to disagree.

The resulting operating hypothesis:

**Target the constraint. Don't subsidise the island.**

---

### 03 — Own St Julian's After 22:00

St Julian's behaves differently from residential Malta.

Tourism.

Nightlife.

Hotels.

Late eating occasions.

Different merchant availability.

Different courier requirements.

So COUNTRY GM treats it as a distinct marketplace problem rather than another geographic row in a national dashboard.

The hypothesis:

**Build an occasion advantage around late-night St Julian's.**

---

# Malta is not one market

COUNTRY GM creates different operating hypotheses for:

- St Julian's
- Sliema
- Gżira
- Valletta
- Msida
- St Paul's Bay
- Gozo

The objective isn't to create a prettier heatmap.

It's to ask:

> **Where should Bolt behave differently?**

A nightlife-heavy tourism zone should not necessarily have the same acquisition, merchant, courier or promotional strategy as a dense residential locality.

And Gozo may deserve a different marketplace playbook entirely.

---

# Merchant Intelligence

A marketplace doesn't win because it has the largest spreadsheet of restaurant logos.

The more useful questions are:

- Which customer occasions are poorly served?
- When does important selection become unavailable?
- Where does merchant quality matter more than merchant quantity?
- Which merchants can anchor an occasion?
- Where does the customer have selection but still lack a reason to choose Bolt?

COUNTRY GM therefore looks at merchants through **occasion × locality × availability × customer type**, rather than only merchant count.

Public Bolt Food listings are used as examples.

No internal merchant performance is claimed.

---

# Growth must survive economics

COUNTRY GM deliberately separates:

**GMV growth**

from

**good growth.**

The P&L Lab asks what happens after:

- incentives
- courier economics
- contribution per order
- customer activation
- repeat behaviour
- service quality

A growth idea that destroys contribution should not survive simply because it increases orders.

# Malta 52

I don't believe a country strategy should be a static annual presentation.

So COUNTRY GM contains a continuous hypothesis engine:

> **Week 17 does not exist yet.**

Each experiment should create information that changes the next decision.

The operating loop is:

**Observe → Investigate → Challenge → Execute → Learn → Reprioritise**

The system continuously asks:

> **Where is the next €1M of profitable opportunity hiding?**

Not because the modeled € figure is a forecast.

It isn't.

It is a mechanism for forcing opportunities to compete for management attention.

# The agents

COUNTRY GM uses four operating agents.

### Market Intelligence Agent

Understands the market.

Looks for external signals, geographic differences, customer behaviour, tourism patterns and competitive clues.

### Growth Agent

Turns signals into opportunities.

Asks where acquisition, frequency, selection or cross-product advantages might exist.

### Economics Agent

Tries to kill them.

Challenges contribution, incentive dependence, operational complexity and assumptions.

### Experiment Agent

Turns surviving hypotheses into tests.

Defines:

- hypothesis
- metric
- cohort
- control
- kill criteria
- scale criteria

The principle is intentionally simple:

> **Agents investigate. Operators decide.**

AI should make investigation dramatically cheaper.

It should not remove accountability.

# Ask COUNTRY GM

The final interface allows an operator to provide a mandate such as:

> Grow Malta 20% without increasing blanket discounting.

COUNTRY GM investigates the constraint, rejects weak approaches and recommends what it would test first.

But importantly, it also answers:

### What would change my recommendation?

Because operating without internal data should create questions — not fake certainty.

For example, I would want access to:

1. Ride → Food customer overlap
2. Contribution per order by cohort
3. Orders and conversion by locality × hour
4. Courier utilisation and ETA by zone × interval
5. Merchant availability and unavailable-search data
6. Bolt Plus penetration and frequency uplift
7. New vs repeat customer economics
8. Competitive delivery experience by locality

Give me the real numbers and I would rerank Malta.

---

# Evidence discipline

There are three deliberately different kinds of information inside COUNTRY GM.

### PUBLIC FACT

Information observable from public Bolt material, Malta government statistics or other identified public sources.

### PUBLIC INFERENCE

A hypothesis derived from multiple public signals.

It should be investigated — not treated as truth.

### MODELED ASSUMPTION

Illustrative economics used to demonstrate how I would reason about a decision.

These are **not Bolt's numbers**.

I have no access to Bolt's internal operating, customer, merchant or financial data.
